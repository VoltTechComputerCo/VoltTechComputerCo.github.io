The Witcher 3 Remastered Is Gorgeous — And PC Already Needed a Rescue Patch

CDPR gave a decade-old RPG path tracing, DLSS 4.5, FSR 4, XeSS 2 and a frankly silly list of upgrades for free. PC then did what PC does best: found the weird stuff immediately.

Published 3 October 2026 · STATIC

Geralt is back on the Path, except this time his hair can be ray traced. Because apparently 2026 demanded that even monster-slaying ponytails participate in the rendering arms race.

The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt — Remastered landed on 29 September as a free upgrade across PC, PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S and Nintendo Switch 2. On PC it is a proper technical overhaul: path tracing, DLSS 4.5 with Ray Reconstruction, FSR 4 with Frame Generation and Anti-Lag 2, XeSS 2, ray-traced HairWorks, improved materials, lighting, terrain and a pile of gameplay changes.

Then launch week happened.

The quick version: CD PROJEKT RED has already pushed Hotfix 5.00c on PC. It fixes a save/load failure involving special characters in the Documents path, repairs several audio and visual issues, tweaks path-traced lighting in Velen and — importantly — rolls back a change that caused major performance and visual problems for some players using HairWorks.

The remaster is much bigger than a texture pack

Calling this a remaster almost undersells it. CDPR rebuilt chunks of the rendering stack around native DirectX 12 multithreading, improved shading, new image-based lighting, reworked bloom, ray-traced local shadows and upgraded terrain and material rendering. The studio also folded assets from the excellent HD Reworked Project into the official release.

PC gets the full buffet. NVIDIA users can push path tracing with DLSS 4.5 and Ray Reconstruction. AMD gets FSR 4, Frame Generation and Anti-Lag 2 on Radeon RX 7000-series cards and newer. Intel gets XeSS 2 with Frame Generation and Low Latency. If your GPU has been sitting around feeling underworked, Geralt has arrived to correct that behaviour.

Hotfix 5.00c fixes the kind of bug you really don't want in an RPG

The headline fix is brutally simple: some PC players could not save or load if their Documents folder path contained special characters. In a 100-hour RPG, the phrase “can't save” has roughly the same calming effect as hearing a Leshen behind you in a dark forest.

The hotfix also fixes voice audio becoming much quieter after moving through pause-menu tabs, restores GOG achievements that were not appearing correctly, and fixes an issue that prevented the classic 1.32 version from launching.

Then there's HairWorks. CDPR says it reverted a change that caused significant performance and visual issues for some players with HairWorks enabled. That is especially funny considering HairWorks was one of the original 2015 game's great “how badly can I hurt my GPU?” buttons. Eleven years later, the hair remains undefeated.

Path tracing is getting cleaned up too

Hotfix 5.00c includes visual adjustments around Velen covering bloom, vegetation lighting, saturation and path-traced lighting. CDPR hasn't framed those as some dramatic rendering rewrite; they're launch-week tuning passes. But on a remaster selling itself partly on its lighting overhaul, they're exactly the kind of fixes worth watching.

Console players got their own patch, 5.00b, on 1 October. CDPR says that update fixed DLSS availability in the Xbox Play Anywhere PC version, adjusted lighting — including interiors that were too dark — and improved parity with the PC release on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S.

And underneath the graphics, CDPR changed the actual game

This is the bit that makes Remastered more interesting than another “look, shinier puddles” update. Skills have been reorganised into a proper tree and rebalanced. Combat behaviour for a range of monsters has been polished. Geralt gets improved movement and animation, new finishers, better climbing and vaulting, while Roach gets revised movement and collision behaviour.

Photo Mode has been massively expanded, meditation now uses a timelapse, nearby loot can be grouped into one window, and cross-platform mod support arrives through mod.io. There's even an accessibility preset designed to reduce cognitive load.

Oh, and you can loot without getting off Roach now. Somewhere, someone who has spent 400 hours picking herbs just shed a single tear.

So should you jump in right now?

If you already own The Witcher 3, the value proposition is hilariously easy: Remastered is a free upgrade in the same platform ecosystem, and CDPR now includes Hearts of Stone and Blood and Wine for owners as part of the package. That's an absurd amount of RPG for zero additional rands, dollars, euros or carefully hoarded crowns.

On PC, though, the first few days are a reminder that huge renderer overhauls tend to shake loose weird edge cases. Hotfix 5.00c deals with several of the nastier ones, and CDPR says it is still investigating reported issues.

STATIC's take: this is the kind of remaster we want to see: free for existing owners, technically ambitious and willing to mess with more than the texture folder. The rough edges are real, but CDPR pushing multiple fixes almost immediately is a much better story than leaving Geralt's radioactive HairWorks to terrorise GPUs for a month.
