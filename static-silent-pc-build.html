Building a Silent PC: The Nerdy Art of Not Hearing Your Computer

A quiet PC is not built by simply buying silent-branded parts. It comes from reducing heat, avoiding turbulence and choosing components that do not need to work so hard.

VOLTTECH COMPUTER CO. · 27 AUG 2026

Silence starts with efficiency

Less heat means fans can spin more slowly. Efficient CPUs and GPUs can therefore matter more than exotic acoustic foam.

Big slow fans are your friends

A larger fan can often move the same amount of air at a lower speed than a small fan, reducing the pitch and intensity of the noise.

Airflow restriction creates noise

Tight mesh, blocked intakes and badly arranged cables force fans to work harder for the same cooling result.

Fan curves matter

Default fan behaviour is often unnecessarily aggressive. A sensible curve can keep a system quiet at idle without sacrificing safe temperatures under load.

Coil whine is different

Electronic whine from a GPU or power supply is not solved by changing case fans. It may vary with frame rate, power load and the individual component.
