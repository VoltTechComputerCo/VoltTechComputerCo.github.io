Xbox Just Made Game Pass Very Hard to Ignore

2026-09-16

Microsoft has revealed the second September wave for Xbox Game Pass, and this one is less about padding a catalogue than putting genuinely expensive, high-profile games directly in front of subscribers.

Dune: Awakening arrives 22 September, Minecraft Dungeons II lands day one on 29 September, and Gears of War: E-Day follows day one on 6 October. Around them are Kernel Hearts, Well Dweller, Mad King Redemption and several games expanding into Game Pass Premium.

Dune is the immediate value play

Funcom's Dune: Awakening joins Game Pass Ultimate, Premium and PC Game Pass on 22 September. On the South African Xbox Store, the base game is listed at R839, with Deluxe at R1,179 and Ultimate at R1,519.

The Xbox version is substantially expanded from the original PC launch. Funcom says it includes full single-player, cross-play and cross-progression, Xbox Play Anywhere, the complete Book One story, optional PvP, difficulty customisation and a Performance Mode targeting 60fps.

Then Minecraft Dungeons II lands day one

On 29 September, Mojang's Minecraft Dungeons II joins Game Pass Ultimate and PC Game Pass at launch. It expands the action-RPG formula with new environments, threats and gear customisation while retaining the accessible co-op identity of the original.

And then comes E-Day

Gears of War: E-Day launches 6 October on Xbox Series X|S and PC and enters Game Pass Ultimate and PC Game Pass on day one. The Coalition takes the series back fourteen years before the original, following Marcus Fenix and Dom Santiago as the Locust first emerge.

Xbox says E-Day includes a solo campaign, two-player split-screen campaign co-op on console, up to four-player online co-op, Horde Siege, competitive multiplayer, modernised traversal and an Unreal Engine 5 foundation targeting up to 4K/60fps.

Not every Game Pass tier gets everything

Dune: Awakening is listed for Ultimate, Premium and PC Game Pass. Minecraft Dungeons II and Gears of War: E-Day are listed for Ultimate and PC Game Pass. Several older additions — including Ninja Gaiden 4 and Call of Duty: Black Ops 6 — expand into Premium during the same wave.

The catch: Xbox is also tightening cloud gaming

From November, Microsoft says included cloud gaming will be capped at 15 hours per month for Ultimate, 10 for Premium and five for Essential, with additional cloud time sold separately. Downloaded console and PC play is therefore a different value proposition from relying heavily on streaming.

For South African gamers, do the maths around what you'll actually play

The useful calculation isn't how many hundreds of games are in the catalogue. It's which games you were genuinely going to buy during the months you stay subscribed. If Dune at R839 and E-Day at roughly R1,299 were already on your shopping list, Game Pass has a much stronger case. If you only want one game and plan to play it for months, ownership may still make more sense.

Three weeks that make the argument

Game Pass doesn't need every month to look like this. It needs enough months where subscribers can point to games they genuinely wanted and say the subscription changed what they had to buy. Between Dune, Minecraft Dungeons II and E-Day, Microsoft has built exactly that kind of stretch.
