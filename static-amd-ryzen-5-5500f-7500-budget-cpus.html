AMD Just Made Budget PC Building Interesting Again

2026-09-14

AMD quietly launched the Ryzen 5 5500F and Ryzen 5 7500 on September 10. Both are six-core, 12-thread, 65W desktop processors, but they are aimed at completely different builders.

The $99 5500F is the one to watch

AMD lists the 5500F as Zen 3 “Vermeer”: six cores, 12 threads, 3.0GHz base, 4.4GHz boost, 16MB L3 and a 65W TDP. It needs a discrete GPU. Crucially, AMD specifies PCIe 4.0, while the older Ryzen 5 5500 is limited to PCIe 3.0.

Major hardware outlets report a $99 US launch price. That makes it potentially compelling for an existing AM4 owner who can reuse a motherboard and DDR4 instead of rebuilding the entire platform.

Do not assume it beats the Ryzen 5 5600

The naming is messy. The new 5500F has only 16MB of L3 cache — half the Ryzen 5 5600’s 32MB. Until independent retail benchmarks arrive, the 5600 should remain the safer performance reference. The 5500F’s argument is price and PCIe 4.0, not a guaranteed performance win.

The Ryzen 5 7500 is a 7500F with useful backup graphics

The AM5 chip is Zen 4 with six cores, 12 threads, 3.7GHz base and 5.0GHz boost, 32MB L3, DDR5 and PCIe 5.0. It also enables AMD’s tiny two-core Radeon iGPU at up to 2.2GHz.

That integrated GPU is for display output, troubleshooting and basic desktop use — not serious gaming. But it is genuinely useful when building or diagnosing a PC.

The platform price matters more than the CPU price

An AM4 upgrader can potentially keep an existing board and DDR4. An AM5 build needs an AM5 motherboard and DDR5, so a cheap CPU does not automatically mean a cheap PC.

The reported $189 US pricing for the 7500 also needs context: discounted Ryzen 7600, 7600X or newer Zen 5 chips can sometimes sit uncomfortably close. Always compare street prices, not model hierarchy.

What this means for South African builders

US MSRP is not a South African shelf price. Distributor stock, import costs and local promotions can completely reorder the value equation. If the 5500F lands cheaply, it could be a particularly useful upgrade for the huge installed base of AM4 gaming PCs. If its local price creeps too close to a 5600, buy the faster chip instead.

For a completely new build, AM5 remains the better long-term platform — but only if the motherboard and DDR5 premium fits the budget.

AM4 turning ten is the real headline

The 5500F is not cutting-edge silicon. That is precisely why it matters. AMD is acknowledging that millions of builders do not need a new socket, new RAM and a premium motherboard to make an older gaming PC meaningfully better.
