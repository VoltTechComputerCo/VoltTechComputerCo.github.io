Why Your PC Throttles Under Load — And How to Actually Fix It

Thermal throttling can make a powerful PC feel broken. Here is how to recognise it, what usually causes it and what to check before buying new hardware.

VOLTTECH COMPUTER CO. · 26 AUG 2026

The pattern that gives throttling away

Performance is normal at first, then drops after several minutes under load. That delay matters: it often means heat is building until the CPU or GPU deliberately reduces speed to protect itself.

Check temperatures before blaming the hardware

Use a monitoring tool while gaming or rendering. Look for a temperature spike that lines up with the performance drop. A hot component is not automatically faulty; the cooling system may be the real problem.

Start with the boring causes

Dust, blocked vents, weak airflow and dried thermal paste are common. A fan that spins is not necessarily moving enough air, and a tidy-looking case can still have clogged heatsink fins.

Do not repaste everything blindly

Thermal paste can help when old material has dried out or a cooler mount is poor, but it is not a cure for every high-temperature reading. Fix airflow and fan behaviour first when they are obviously wrong.

What a good fix looks like

The goal is not the lowest possible temperature. It is stable performance, sensible temperatures and fan noise that matches the workload.

The useful answer is usually less dramatic than the internet argument around it.
