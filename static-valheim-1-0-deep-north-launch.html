Valheim 1.0 Launches Today — Deep North Ends a Five-Year Early Access Saga

Valheim is scheduled to leave Early Access today, September 9. Iron Gate’s 1.0 release adds the long-awaited Deep North, expands the platform list to PlayStation 5 and Nintendo Switch 2, and turns one of PC gaming’s defining survival hits into a fully cross-platform release.

STATIC · 9 SEP 2026 · NEWS / ANALYSIS

Valheim first entered Early Access on 2 February 2021. More than five and a half years later, Iron Gate says version 1.0 arrives on 9 September 2026 across Windows, Linux, Mac, Xbox One, Xbox Series X|S, PlayStation 5 and Nintendo Switch 2, with full crossplay between platforms.

That makes today more than a normal content update. The Deep North is the final biome update Iron Gate has been building toward as the point where Valheim becomes a 1.0 game. It also arrives with achievements, a higher full-release price, and a much larger addressable audience on Sony and Nintendo hardware.

STATIC signal: “launches today” does not necessarily mean “live everywhere at this exact moment.” Iron Gate’s official pages confirm the September 9 date, but the cited release and FAQ pages do not specify one universal unlock time. The important confirmed point is that September 9 is the scheduled 1.0 release date.

Deep North is not just another biome

Iron Gate has described Deep North as the biome update that brings Valheim into 1.0. Earlier development posts have shown some of the practical systems that make the region more than a snowy reskin: harvestable lingonberries, slippery ice, deep snow that has to be cleared, and a craftable shovel for settling the frozen landscape.

Those details matter because Valheim’s best biomes change how players prepare, travel and build rather than simply changing enemy health bars. Swamps force poison management and awkward traversal; Mountains make cold resistance part of progression; Mistlands changes visibility and movement. Deep North has the same opportunity to make the environment itself the mechanic.

Your old worlds and saves are not being deleted

The biggest practical question for long-time players has a reassuring answer. Iron Gate’s 1.0 FAQ says existing saves will remain available and old worlds will receive new content. However, biome generation works properly only in areas that have not already been explored.

That is why the studio still recommends starting a new playthrough for the best 1.0 experience. You do not have to wipe years of work, but a heavily explored world can limit how naturally newly generated biome content appears.

Valheim becomes a bigger platform game at exactly the right moment

Valheim has spent most of its life as a PC-and-Xbox phenomenon. Version 1.0 adds PlayStation 5 and Nintendo Switch 2 on day one of the full release, while keeping crossplay intact. That gives established communities a much easier way to bring new players into existing groups without forcing everyone onto the same hardware.

The game remains built around one-to-ten-player survival, and Iron Gate says independent player-hosted servers remain part of the core design. Crossplay therefore matters more here than it would in a mostly solo game: the social architecture of Valheim is one of the reasons worlds stay active for months or years.

The price changes too

Iron Gate says the full-release price moves to $29.99, with regional differences possible. Existing Early Access owners do not need to buy the game again; their copy updates normally. The estimated download is roughly 4.3 GiB on most platforms, with Xbox One around 3.0 GiB and Mac App Store / Microsoft Store versions around 6.5 GiB.

Achievements are also finally arriving. The studio says achievement tracking begins from the moment players install 1.0, so old saves remain usable, but historical statistics are not retroactively reconstructed from everything you did during Early Access.

Why 1.0 matters even if you already played hundreds of hours

Valheim is one of those Early Access games that already felt culturally “released” years before version 1.0. That makes today unusual: the label is catching up to a game that already has a huge player history rather than introducing an unknown project to the world.

The meaningful test is whether Deep North gives veteran players a real endgame reason to return while the PS5 and Switch 2 launches create a new wave of first-time players. If both happen at once, Valheim’s 1.0 moment could behave less like the end of Early Access and more like a second launch.
