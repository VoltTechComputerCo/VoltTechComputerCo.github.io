Kitsune Conquered Comic Con Africa — Inside the VS Gaming Masters 2026 Aftermath

Kitsune took the trophy. HighBorn made their statement. And inside a packed Comic Con Africa, South African Counter-Strike got one of its biggest stages of the year.

Published 1 October 2026 · STATIC

Johannesburg had a Counter-Strike arena for a weekend. Between the cosplay, comics, hardware, anime and thousands of people moving through the Johannesburg Expo Centre, eight of South Africa's strongest Counter-Strike 2 teams arrived at Comic Con Africa with R340,000 on the line.

By Sunday evening, Kitsune Esports were the 2026 Telkom VS Gaming Masters champions after beating HighBorn 3–0 in the best-of-five grand final.

Final standings: Kitsune — 1st, R150,000 · HighBorn — 2nd, R70,000 · PhaZe — 3rd, R40,000 · Crimson Fang — 4th, R20,000 · Realms Esports, Omnius Gaming, Synthesis and PREZTiGE — 5th–8th, R15,000 each.

Kitsune finished the job

Kitsune's championship five — Doru, wilj, Triton, March and FROZ3N — arrived with experience and delivered when the tournament reached its biggest stage. Telkom's post-event account described a composed performance across all three maps, while wilj said the team's preparation through the year had been central to the win and that, for most of the roster, this was a third Masters victory.

The 3–0 score was decisive, but it wasn't the whole story of the domestic season. HighBorn had beaten Kitsune 3–1 in the ACGL Season 4 final in July; Kitsune then beat HighBorn 2–0 in the South African ENC qualifier three days later. Comic Con became the latest chapter of a matchup that had already moved both ways online.

HighBorn made their statement too

A 3–0 final can make second place look further away than it really was. HighBorn — DropShot, wipeout, Cloudzehh, burnz and Natural — still finished ahead of six other Masters teams and earned R70,000. Their earlier ACGL win over Kitsune also showed this was not a roster that simply stumbled into the final.

The result gives the local scene something it needs: another team capable of pushing the established names, creating rematches and giving domestic Counter-Strike a storyline that extends beyond one weekend.

A quick personal note: I've known Cloudzehh a little since at least 2022, when I played against his team in a qualifier for a spot in the VS Gaming Premier Division. We lost. What I remember is that they were simply the stronger, more confident team on the day. So seeing him several years later IGL HighBorn all the way to second place at the VS Gaming Masters at Comic Con Africa was pretty cool to watch from a personal perspective. Anyway — enough nostalgia. There was a lot more Counter-Strike happening at Comic Con.

The rest of the Top 8 mattered

PhaZe took third and R40,000, while Crimson Fang finished fourth for R20,000. Realms Esports, Omnius Gaming, Synthesis and PREZTiGE completed the eight-team field. PREZTiGE's lineup included splitsecond, tweaKzx, Nando, Mattyzzzbruh and FreezySL — another reminder of how much recognisable local talent was packed into the bracket.

We looked for a complete, trustworthy set of player-by-player statistics from the tournament and could not verify one across every map. So STATIC isn't going to manufacture an MVP ranking from incomplete numbers. Where the record is clear, the team results speak loudly enough.

Comic Con was much bigger than the bracket

Comic Con Africa ran from 24–27 September at the Johannesburg Expo Centre in Nasrec. Gaming occupied Hall 6, an area of more than 7,000 square metres, with esports alongside free-to-play stations, sim racing, hardware, new releases and other activations.

The VS Gaming programme stretched beyond Masters CS2. Counter-Strike, Rocket League and Tekken competitions contributed to a combined advertised prize pool of R547,500. St John's College won the 2026 High School Esports League Counter-Strike championship, while the wider hall mixed professional competition with hands-on gaming.

Post-event reporting put total Comic Con Africa attendance above 72,000 people, with Saturday sold out. Not all of them stood in front of the Counter-Strike stage. That's not the point. Competitive South African gaming existed inside the same cultural space as cosplay, anime, comics, celebrities, collectibles and technology.

That crossover is the real opportunity

An online Counter-Strike tournament mostly reaches people who already know they want to watch Counter-Strike. Comic Con can put the same match in front of someone who arrived for something completely different.

You could walk through Hall 6 to try a racing rig or play a free game and find South Africa's best CS2 teams fighting for a national title on a huge stage. For a local scene that has always battled geography, limited international opposition and a smaller professional ecosystem, that visibility matters.

South African Counter-Strike still has something worth watching

South African Counter-Strike is small compared with Europe. The distances are worse, the international opportunities are fewer and the professional market is thinner. None of that makes the domestic competition meaningless.

Eight teams qualified for a R340,000 offline championship. HighBorn showed during the season that Kitsune could be beaten. Kitsune answered when the biggest domestic trophy was on the line. Players who spend months grinding local leagues got to compete inside one of Africa's biggest pop-culture events.

That's how scenes keep moving: players get a reason to practise, teams get something worth qualifying for, fans get rivalries worth following, and new players get to see what the top of the local ladder actually looks like.

STATIC's take: Kitsune leave Nasrec as deserved champions. HighBorn leave with a runner-up finish that matters more than the 0–3 final score suggests. Comic Con Africa leaves behind the bigger reminder: South African esports is at its best when the competition and the culture occupy the same room.
