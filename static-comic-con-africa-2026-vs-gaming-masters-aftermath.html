Kitsune Conquered Comic Con Africa — Inside the VS Gaming Masters 2026 Aftermath

2026-10-01

For one weekend, Joburg had a proper Counter-Strike arena again. Somewhere between the cosplay, anime merch, celebrities, PC hardware and the bloke inevitably carrying a sword twice his own height, eight of South Africa's strongest CS2 teams were busy fighting over R340,000 at Comic Con Africa.

And when the dust settled on Sunday, Kitsune Esports were standing on top. They swept HighBorn 3–0 in the best-of-five grand final and walked out of Nasrec as the 2026 Telkom VS Gaming Masters champions.

Kitsune finished the job

Kitsune's championship five — Doru, wilj, Triton, March and FROZ3N — had the experience, and when the biggest match of the weekend arrived they looked completely at home. Three maps, three wins, trophy secured. Telkom's post-event coverage described a composed performance, while wilj credited the work the team had put in throughout the year.

That 3–0 can make the rivalry look one-sided if you only saw the final. It wasn't. HighBorn beat Kitsune 3–1 in the ACGL Season 4 final in July, then Kitsune hit back 2–0 in the South African ENC qualifier just three days later. By the time they met at Comic Con, there was already a bit of history simmering. LAN just turned the stove up.

HighBorn made their statement too

Getting 3–0'd in a grand final hurts. There is no clever way around that. But HighBorn — DropShot, wipeout, Cloudzehh, burnz and Natural — still beat their way past six other Masters teams, reached the final and took home R70,000. More importantly, their earlier win over Kitsune proved they weren't there by accident.

That's good for SA Counter-Strike. Nobody wants a scene where you can fill in the winner before the server even loads. HighBorn have given Kitsune another team to worry about, and given the rest of us another matchup worth actually tuning in for.

The rest of the Top 8 mattered

PhaZe grabbed third and R40,000, with Crimson Fang taking fourth and R20,000. Realms Esports, Omnius Gaming, Synthesis and PREZTiGE rounded out the Top 8. PREZTiGE alone had splitsecond, tweaKzx, Nando, Mattyzzzbruh and FreezySL in the server — plenty of familiar names if you've spent any time following local CS.

There isn't a clean public stat sheet covering every player across every map, so the juiciest story here is still the one we could all see clearly: Kitsune got the trophy, HighBorn pushed into the final, and the local pecking order suddenly feels a lot more interesting heading out of Comic Con.

Comic Con was much bigger than the bracket

Of course, Comic Con Africa isn't exactly known for doing things quietly. From 24–27 September, the Johannesburg Expo Centre in Nasrec became four days of costumes, comics, gaming, collectibles, tech, queues, noise and the sort of glorious nerd overload that makes your wallet nervous.

Gaming alone took over more than 7,000 square metres of Hall 6. Alongside the esports stage were free-to-play stations, sim racing, hardware, new releases and enough blinking RGB to probably be visible from Pretoria.

The VS Gaming programme stretched well beyond Masters CS2 too. Counter-Strike, Rocket League and Tekken competitions contributed to a combined advertised prize pool of R547,500 . St John's College won the 2026 High School Esports League Counter-Strike championship, while the rest of the hall blurred the line between serious competition and simply getting your hands on something fun.

Post-event reporting put total Comic Con Africa attendance above 72,000 people , with Saturday sold out. Obviously not all 72,000 were shoulder-to-shoulder watching Counter-Strike — imagine the smell — but that's exactly why having the tournament there matters.

People came for Comic Con. Some left having watched SA Counter-Strike.

An online CS tournament mostly reaches people who already care enough to find the stream. Comic Con is different. Someone can arrive dressed as Gojo, wander into Hall 6 looking for a racing rig and suddenly find two South African teams screaming comms at each other on a national stage.

That's the crossover local esports needs. South Africa has always had talented Counter-Strike players; what we don't have is Europe's endless conveyor belt of nearby opponents, LANs and organisations. Geography is basically playing permanent hard mode against us.

SA Counter-Strike is still lekker to watch

We're not Europe. We don't have a massive international event every other weekend, and our best teams can't hop on a two-hour flight and scrim half the world's elite. But that doesn't make what happens here any less worth caring about.

Eight teams qualified for a R340,000 offline championship. HighBorn proved during the season that Kitsune could bleed. Kitsune answered by absolutely shutting the door when the biggest domestic trophy was on the line. And a crowd full of gamers, cosplayers and curious passers-by got to see local CS played under proper LAN lights.

That's the stuff that keeps a scene alive. Give players something worth grinding for. Give teams rivals they actually want to beat. Give fans names and rematches to argue about. Then put the whole thing somewhere a 15-year-old Counter-Strike player can look at the stage and think, hell, I want to be up there next year.

STATIC's take: Kitsune leave Nasrec as champions and deservedly so. HighBorn leave with a runner-up finish that feels more important than the ugly 0–3 beside it. And Comic Con once again proved something South African gaming occasionally forgets: esports is way more fun when it feels like part of the culture instead of a tournament happening quietly in another browser tab.
